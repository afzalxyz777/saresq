How to use this. Each block below is one slide. On slide is the text that goes on screen — keep it short, it is not a document. Say is your spoken line. Visual is what to build. Every number here is measured and traceable to the technical report.

The narrative arc: drone search is already possible — but a human has to watch the video. That human is the bottleneck. We put the watching onboard. Doing that on a ₹4,000 computer is the hard engineering, and we have the measurements to prove we did it.

03

The real bottleneck

The insight — most important slide
On slide

The drone isn't the bottleneck. The person watching the feed is.

  • Waypoint flight has been solved for a decade
  • A survivor is a 2.6-pixel warm smudge on a low-resolution thermal feed at 4 frames a second
  • Human visual search degrades sharply with time on task
  • An operator cannot rewind, cannot combine three weak sightings, cannot attach a GPS fix to a hunch
Say: "We are not automating the flying. We are automating the looking — because that is the part that fails."
Visual
An actual 32×24 thermal frame with a person in it, blown up. Let the audience try to spot them. Then reveal the box. This lands hard — it makes the difficulty physical rather than asserted.

How it works

Architecture — keep it to 40 seconds
On slide

Look cheaply everywhere. Look expensively almost nowhere.

StageDoesCost
1. Thermal gateFlags warm or cold anomalies in a 32×24 frame. Pure statistics, no AI.~0 ms
2. DetectorAI looks only at the 2–6 flagged patches28 ms each

Say: "A full frame is 409,600 pixels. A patch is 25,600. We do about a sixteenth of the work a normal detector would — that is the entire reason this runs on a ₹4,000 computer instead of a laptop."

A horizontal 4-box pipeline, each box labelled with its cost. Animate left to right if the format allows.
06

Cold matters too

Depth — shows you thought past the obvious
On slide

We look for cold anomalies as well as warm ones.

  • A body in water reads colder than its surroundings
  • A void beneath rubble reads colder than sun-baked concrete
  • In direct afternoon sun, debris can be hotter than a human being — a "find hot things" system fails exactly then
Say: "A naive thermal detector looks for hot spots. In Kolkata sun, the rubble is the hot spot. So our gate is two-sided — and we recommend flying at dawn, dusk or night. That's a physics constraint, not a software one."

This slide is optional if time is tight — but it is the kind of detail that separates a project that was thought about from one that was assembled.

We measured the obvious choices — and two of them were wrong.

This is the strongest slide in the deck. It demonstrates engineering judgement, not just a working demo — and every judge has seen projects that would have shipped that bug.

Act IV — Fit and future

10

It fits your drone

Feasibility
On slide

Bolts to any 450 mm-class quadcopter. No firmware change. No re-certification.

  • 4 × M3 into a standard vibration-damping plate, or 2 straps through the built-in slots
  • Own battery, own GPS, own IMU — zero electrical connection to the flight controller
  • Flat layout keeps the centre of gravity low; prints with no supports
  • GPS antenna on a separate mast — a Pi radiates across the GPS band at 1575 MHz
Say: "Because it never touches the flight stack, it cannot brown it out and cannot cause a flyaway. Fitting it to a new airframe is a bolt-on, not an integration project."
Visual
The CAD render or a photo of the printed tray with the mounting holes called out.

Reserve slides — for Q&A

R1

What we know doesn't work yet

Answer this before they ask
On slide

Honest limits

  • Never flown. Benchmarked on the board, not in the air.
  • The evidence-fusion stage is weak (0.67 AUC) — 5 of its 18 inputs need sensors mounted and flying.
  • No daytime visible detector. Thermal-only today; the dataset we had for visible turned out to be night-time imagery with no usable signal.
  • Search-coverage figure withheld — the only measurement available is out-of-domain, and publishing it would overstate coverage roughly 5×.
Say: "We'd rather tell you the weak parts than have you find them."
R2

Best operating altitude

Likely question
On slide

20–30 m. The limit is the thermal sensor, not the AI.

AltitudeThermal pixels on a prone adultSwath
20 m2.121 m
25 m1.426 m
40 m0.5 — contrast halves42 m

Below one thermal pixel the person's heat averages with the cold ground around them, and apparent contrast falls as 1/height². The detector barely degrades over this range — the sensor does.

R3

Why not just use a bigger model / a Jetson?

Likely challenge
On slide

Because the constraint is the contribution.

  • A Jetson is 5–10× the cost and draws far more power — which is flight time
  • Anyone can run a large model on a large computer; that isn't deployable at scale in a disaster response budget
  • Our model is 3.2 MB and uses 95 MB of RAM — a state relief agency can buy fifty of these
Say: "The engineering question isn't 'can AI find people in thermal images' — it's 'can it do that on hardware a state disaster response force can actually afford to buy in quantity'."
