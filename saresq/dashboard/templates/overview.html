SaResQ

A thermal + visible camera payload that finds people from a small drone, decides on the aircraft whether what it sees is a person, and sends the answer to the ground over a radio that is too slow for video.
Raspberry Pi 4 · MLX90640 · Pi Camera v2 · checking payload…

The problem, and the idea

After an earthquake or a flood, the slow part of a rescue is not travelling — it is searching. A drone can cover a collapsed block in minutes, but it comes back with an hour of video that a person still has to watch.

SaResQ watches instead. A thermal camera says where to look, a normal camera says what is there, and only the few frames that contain a person are ever sent anywhere. The reason it is built this way is a hard limit: the radio a small drone carries moves about 16 kilobits per second. A single video frame takes thirteen seconds on it. A map pin takes fourteen milliseconds.

How it works

  1. 1

    The thermal camera looks for anything warmer than its surroundings

    Not “warmer than 30 °C” — there is no such fixed number. It compares every pixel against the median of the frame, scaled by the median absolute deviation, and flags anything 2.5 standard deviations above it. Median and MAD are used instead of mean and standard deviation because a person in the frame would otherwise inflate the very threshold meant to find them.

  2. 2

    Those hot spots become crops of the normal camera

    The thermal array is only 32×24 — a person at 20 m is about 2.6 pixels. That is enough to say “something warm is there”, never enough to say “that is a person”. A calibrated affine transform maps the hot spot into the 1640×1232 visible frame, where the same person is roughly 115×31 pixels.

  3. 3

    A neural network judges only those crops

    Running the detector on every full frame would not fit the Pi’s budget. Running it on six 160 px crops costs 169 ms of a 250 ms frame. The thermal camera has done the expensive part — deciding where to spend the compute.

  4. 4

    A second network reads the scene as a whole

    Separately, once a second, the whole frame goes to a classifier that says whether this is a flood, a fire, a collapsed building, a traffic incident, or a normal scene. A survivor in a flooded street and one on dry ground are different dispatch decisions.

  5. 5

    Only the answer is transmitted

    A confirmed detection becomes a 28-byte alert — position, class, confidence — that arrives almost instantly, with the confirming crop following a couple of seconds later. Full video never goes over the radio, because it cannot.

What this does not do

  • It does not see through rubble. Thermal reads surface temperature. A person under a slab is invisible to it; what it finds is someone exposed, or a warm gap.
  • It has no fix indoors. The GPS needs sky. Captures without a fix carry no coordinates, and an operator-set datum is drawn dashed and labelled so it can never be read as a measurement.
  • It does not stream video at range. Thirteen seconds a frame on the radio. Live video works over Wi-Fi, within a few hundred metres.
  • The detector is not certain. AP50 of 0.85 on aerial thermal means it misses things. That is why a human confirms every find in the review queue.
