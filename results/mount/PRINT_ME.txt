SaResQ printed parts -- what to hand the shop
=============================================
Generated by  tools/make_payload_cad.py   (the ONLY source of truth)
Rebuild with  ./.venv/bin/python tools/make_payload_cad.py --material pla

All three parts verified 2026-09-10: watertight, manifold, no degenerate
faces, ONE connected piece each. The script exits non-zero otherwise, so a
successful run is the go-ahead to print.


MATERIAL: PLA  -----------------------------------------------------------

  PLA is fine for these prints. Ask for WHITE or LIGHT GREY, not black.

  Colour is the single most important choice here and it costs nothing.
  PLA softens from about 55 C; a black part in Kolkata sun passes that, and
  PLA does not snap when it gets there -- it slowly creeps under load, so
  the tray would sag and quietly move the two cameras out of alignment. A
  white part reflects most of that heat and never gets close.

  PLA is actually BETTER than PETG for this job in two ways: it holds
  dimensions more accurately (so the board pockets come out closer to
  nominal), and it warps less on a large flat plate like the tray.

  If the shop offers ABS instead -- decline. On a 145 x 135 mm flat plate
  ABS lifts at the corners without a heated enclosure. ASA or PETG-CF would
  be fine if they have either.


PRINT ORDER  -------------------------------------------------------------

1.  saresq_sensor_mount.stl      72 x 36 x 3 mm    ~2.5 g    ~10 min

    PRINT THIS FIRST, ON ITS OWN. A fit check, not a flight part. It is cut
    from the SAME pocket, aperture and screw-slot geometry as the tray's
    sensor band, so if the MLX90640 and the Pi Camera drop into this, they
    drop into the tray. A few rupees of filament to avoid discovering a
    0.4 mm pocket error in a multi-hour print.

    Check when it comes off the bed:
      - both boards seat flat in their pockets and do not rock
      - the M2 slots line up with the real holes on YOUR boards
      - the lens barrels clear the apertures (11 mm thermal, 9 mm camera)
      - ribbon and jumpers exit through the rear reliefs without folding

    If the boards are TIGHT, it is elephant's foot (see settings below),
    not the model. Ask them to enable the compensation and reprint.

2.  saresq_payload_tray.stl     145 x 135 x 9 mm   ~23.4 g   several hours

    The main part. Carries the thermal camera, Pi Camera, IMU, GPS module,
    Raspberry Pi 4 and the 10000 mAh power bank. Only print this once the
    fit check above has passed.

3.  saresq_gps_mast.stl          32 x 32 x 51 mm   ~5.7 g

    Holds the NEO-6M ceramic patch antenna up and away from the Pi, which
    radiates broadband RF right across the GPS L1 band at 1575 MHz.

    Printed total 29.1 g. Full payload with hardware ~340 g.


PRINT SETTINGS  ----------------------------------------------------------
  Material     PLA, white or light grey
  Layer        0.2 mm
  Infill       25%
  Perimeters   4        (PLA is brittle; the extra shell protects the screw
                         bosses and standoffs on a hard landing)
  Supports     NONE     -- every part prints flat with no bridges
  Orientation  flat on the bed, as modelled
  Elephant's foot compensation   0.15 - 0.20 mm     <-- IMPORTANT

  That last one matters: the board pockets open onto the BED, so first-layer
  squish pushes the pocket walls inward and eats the 0.4 mm clearance. Most
  slicers have this setting (PrusaSlicer/Orca: "Elephant foot compensation";
  Cura: "Initial Layer Horizontal Expansion", set to -0.15 mm).


LATER  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  These PLA parts are good for the SIH submission, which is judged on the
  deck and video with no flight. Before the December Grand Finale, reprint
  the tray in PETG or ASA -- by then it flies and sits in the sun between
  sorties. The geometry does not change, only the filament.


STEP files sit beside each STL. Give the shop the STL; keep the STEP, it is
the editable model and opens in Fusion, SolidWorks or FreeCAD.

If the shop's slicer shows ANY red faces or reports a non-manifold mesh, the
file is not the one listed here -- do not let them "repair" it, re-run the
script instead.
